# Plan: cab-ride videos at each station

Status: **proposed 2026-11-22**, nothing built yet.

The maintainer's request: link the YouTube cab-ride videos, with the moment each station appears,
which the maintainer built with Cowork.

## The database

The database is on the branch `data/cab-ride-videos`, in `cab_ride/db/`. Its
`README_cab_rides.md` describes it, and its `schema.sql` documents every column.

**Size:**
- 4,597 videos in 40 countries, and 30,674 stops along them.
- 3,850 videos are meant to be shown (`app_scope` core or extended). The other 747 are clips,
  vlogs and the like, marked `out`.
- Most shown videos per country: Germany 519, Italy 276, Great Britain 250, Switzerland 217,
  Netherlands 207, Spain 197, France 190.

**Every video was confirmed on YouTube on 2026-11-21.**

**How good the timestamps are** (the 3,850 shown videos):

| Quality | Videos | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| precise | 1,075 | the uploader's chapters mark each station |
| approximate | 48 | sparse or rounded chapters |
| estimated | 1,672 | the order of stops is known, but the times are evenly spaced |
| none | 1,055 | no stops yet |

1,308 videos have only their first and last station, parsed from the title.

**The files:**
- **The tables, three ways:** `videos.csv`, `video_stops.csv` and `stations.csv`; a SQLite copy
  (`cab_rides.db`, 9.9 MB); and app bundles in JSON (Spain 169 rides, about 38 kB gzipped).
- **The scripts** that rebuilt it all from the research documents (`source/`).
- **`GAPS_table.md`:** networks with no video yet.

**Not done yet:**
- The stations aren't matched to the app's stations: `network_stop_id`, `lat` and `lon` are empty.
- In the rest of Europe, country and category were set automatically from titles.

## What to bring into the app

- **The master copy.** The three CSVs plus a reviewed `aliases.csv` become the master copy, in
  `refdata/cabrides/`. They're text, so every change shows up in a pull request. The SQLite file
  and the JSON bundles are generated by the build, not committed (AGENTS.md: no generated files).
- **The scripts** that matter for upkeep move to `tools/cabrides/`. The one-off parsers of the
  research documents stay on the data branch as a record.
- **New videos** are rows added to the CSVs.

## Build

1. **Match the stations,** once per country, kept in `aliases.csv`.
   - First, normalise our station names the way the database does: lower case, no accents, no
     punctuation, no "estación de". Then match on `station_key`.
   - Otherwise, a fuzzy match (token set ratio ≥ 90), only among stations near the video's other
     matched stops.
   - Loose names (a freight "Madrid", a yard, a junction) are decided by hand.
   - Metro and tram stops (`m:` keys) are skipped where the app has no metro.
   - The build lists what it couldn't match.
2. **Better times for "estimated" videos.**
   - The anchors are the stops with a real chapter time, plus the start and the end.
   - Between two anchors, each stop gets a time in proportion to its distance along the line. The
     app's timetables already hold each stop's distance (`dist_m`).
   - That replaces even spacing, and is marked `distance_estimate`. The app shows those times with
     "≈".
3. **Output:** `cabrides.json` per country, next to that country's data (Spain's about 38 kB
   gzipped). The app loads it the first time a station or train sheet needs it.
4. **Weekly check:** a workflow asks YouTube's public oEmbed address (no key) for every video, and
   hides those that are gone or private.

## App

- **Station sheet:** "Vídeos desde la cabina".
  - Up to three, then "ver más".
  - Order: core before extended; precise, then approximate, then estimated; cab, then front, then
    passenger view.
  - Each row: the title, the channel, and a "▶ 41:10" chip, with "≈" when the time is estimated.
  - It opens `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=<id>&t=<s>s`, in the YouTube app if installed.
- **Train sheet:** "Ver desde la cabina".
  - Look for videos that pass both the train's previous stop A and its next stop B, with at most
    three stops between them; same direction first.
  - Time = t(A) + progress × (t(B) − t(A)), shown with "≈", so the video opens about where the train
    is.
  - Otherwise, a video through A or B.
- **Journeys:** the same link on each leg a video covers.
- **Videos with only their two ends** show at those two stations only.
- **No thumbnails, no embedded player.**
  - YouTube's thumbnails and player load from Google's servers, which sends each user's IP address
    to Google and would need a CSP change. In the EU, that kind of loading has been treated as
    needing consent.
  - So rows are text, and the video opens in YouTube.
  - Thumbnails could be an opt-in setting later.
- **Countries:**
  - Spain and Great Britain first: the countries the app has, with the most precise timestamps.
  - Each new country's videos come with that country, after a look at its automatic
    classification.

## Keeping it current

- **No more automatic reading of YouTube's pages.** Some of the enrichment so far read YouTube's
  pages directly, until YouTube's rate limit stopped it. YouTube's terms don't allow automated
  reading other than through its official interfaces.
- **For chapters,** use:
  - the **YouTube Data API**: a free key, 10,000 units a day. One call reads 50 videos' descriptions,
    where chapters are, so all 4,597 take under 100 calls;
  - or oEmbed, which gives title and channel only.
- The API key would be a new secret (`YOUTUBE_API_KEY`), for the maintainer to approve.
- The API's own terms then apply to how we store and show that data. Read them before using it.

## Decisions for the maintainer

1. **Free or Pro.** Proposed: free, because it brings people to the app.
2. **The master copy:** the CSVs in the repository (proposed), or the SQLite file as now.
3. **The data branch.** Proposed: don't merge it as it is. Bring the CSVs, the aliases and the upkeep
   scripts in with V1, and leave the SQLite file, the generated JSON and the research parsers on
   the branch.
4. **Thumbnails:** none (proposed), or an opt-in setting.
5. **A YouTube Data API key** for reading chapters.
